Projects

Machining

Burrs in a returned compressor, stopped at the drill

Before
Burrs from drilling stayed in the block and reached a customer.
After
An air purge clears the holes at the drilling station.
How it was done
Problem
A compressor came back from a customer as faulty. I cut it open and found metal burrs and chips lodged between the block bore and the piston.
Trace
The burrs were coming from the block, through its leg holes. I spent a week standing at the line to see where and how they formed. On the same machine, drill bits sometimes broke in the head-bolt holes, and nothing caught it before the line stopped.
Fix
I proposed an air-purge system: one needle for the head holes outside the machine and one for the leg holes inside it, so chips are blown clear before the block moves on.
Result
100% burr removal from the small holes, at the station that creates them.

Machining

Whole-drill changes replaced with indexable tips

Before
A worn drill meant changing the whole tool and re-aligning it.
After
Only the worn tip is changed, at the machine.
How it was done
Problem
When a drill wore out, the operator changed the whole tool and re-aligned it. Every change cost production time, and alignment errors cost parts.
Fix
After researching the options, I moved the large facing, milling and cutting drills to tip-based tooling, where only the worn tip is replaced. On other tools I changed the flute angle and specifications to extend tool life. I supervised the trial runs until the process was stable.
Result
Faster changeovers, fewer alignment problems, lower tooling cost and less scrap, with part quality held.

Plant-wide

Six local vendors in place of imports

Before
Tools and consumables were imported.
After
Six audited local vendors supply filter paper, drill bits, screws and brushes.
How it was done
Problem
Many tools and consumables were imported, which made them expensive and slow to replace.
Fix
I inspected and audited vendor companies against Samsung policy and quality standards, and registered six. Filter paper, drill bits, screws and brushes moved to local supply. I replaced imported superfinishing tape with an alternative from Saint-Gobain, matched the coolant to the same specification through a vendor, and completed MSDS training to register a chemical supplier. I also coordinated the weekly localization meeting.
Result
Lower cost across tools and consumables, and shorter changeovers on the superfinishing tape. I presented the program and its benefits to the CEO at the annual display meeting.

Leak test

A sealing cap in place of imported leak-test couplers

Before
Three imported couplers per compressor, fitted while the conveyor moved.
After
One coupler and two caps designed in-house.
How it was done
Problem
Each compressor took three couplers before the leak-test tank. Operators fitted them by lever while the hanging conveyor kept moving, which damaged the pipe on the lower shell. Coupler spares had to be imported from Korea.
Fix
The N2 charging port had to keep its coupler, so I designed a cap for the other two ports. We ran a one-month trial during production without affecting output. Early caps came off under N2 pressure in the tank, which let water in and scrapped the compressor, so I revised the design until it held.
Result
Less scrap, less production loss and lower cost, with less coupler repair work on those two ports.

Plant-wide

AGVs in place of forklifts, from collisions to a pallet train

Before
Forklifts carried material from the warehouse to the floor.
After
AGVs pull pallet trains along marked routes.
How it was done
Problem
Material moved from the warehouse to the shop floor by forklift. AGVs were planned, but the routes did not exist yet.
Fix
Working from the plant layout, I marked the guide-strip paths the AGV follows and the stations where it stops. In trial runs it had accidents and could pull only one pallet. We worked through both problems over repeated trials.
Result
The AGVs run without accidents and pull several pallets as a train on stronger motors, which reduced material-handling labor and raised the plant's automation rate.

Machining

Warm-up time moved out of the shift

Before
Machines warmed up after the shift had started.
After
Warm-up is done before the shift starts.
How it was done
Problem
The machining centers are large and take time to warm up. Warming them up at the start of the shift meant lost production time every day.
Fix
With my team, I proposed starting the machines before production time. With management approval, we scheduled some operators to arrive early and begin the warm-up.
Result
Warm-up is finished before the shift begins, so it no longer comes out of production time.
